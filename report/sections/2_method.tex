\section{Method}

\paragraph{DTW and soft-DTW.}
Let $x\in\R^{n\times p}$, $y\in\R^{m\times p}$ be two time series and $\Delta(x,y)=[\|x_i-y_j\|^2]_{ij}\in\R^{n\times m}$ their cost matrix. An alignment is a binary matrix $A\in\mathcal A_{n,m}$ encoding a path from $(1,1)$ to $(n,m)$ made of $\downarrow$ ($x$ advances, $y_j$ is repeated), $\rightarrow$ ($y$ advances) and $\searrow$ (both advance) steps; $|\mathcal A_{n,m}|$ is a Delannoy number, exponential in $n,m$. With the soft-minimum $\minop(a_1,\dots,a_k)=-\gamma\log\sum_i e^{-a_i/\gamma}$ ($\min$ when $\gamma=0$),
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{dtw}(x,y)=\min_{A\in\mathcal A_{n,m}}\langle A,\Delta\rangle,\qquad
\sdtw(x,y)=\minop_{A\in\mathcal A_{n,m}}\langle A,\Delta\rangle .
\end{equation}
Because $\minop$ is a log-sum-exp, $\sdtw$ is smooth (infinitely differentiable) in $\Delta$, hence in $x$, whereas $\mathrm{dtw}$ has kinks wherever the optimal path changes. Two facts used later: $\mathrm{dtw}-\gamma\log|\mathcal A_{n,m}|\le\sdtw\le\mathrm{dtw}$, so $\sdtw\to\mathrm{dtw}$ as $\gamma\to0$; and $\sdtw(x,x)<0$, i.e.\ soft-DTW is not a divergence~\cite{blondel2021differentiable}.

\paragraph{Forward pass (Algorithm 1).}
Both quantities are computed by Bellman's recursion, $r_{0,0}=0$, $r_{i,0}=r_{0,j}=+\infty$,
\begin{equation}
r_{i,j}=\delta_{i,j}+\minop\{r_{i-1,j-1},\,r_{i-1,j},\,r_{i,j-1}\},\qquad \sdtw(x,y)=r_{n,m},
\end{equation}
in $O(nm)$ time; $\minop$ is evaluated with the log-sum-exp trick. The whole table $R=[r_{i,j}]$ is stored for the backward pass.

\paragraph{Gradient (Algorithm 2).}
By the chain rule, $\nabla_x\sdtw=(\partial\Delta/\partial x)^\top E$ with $E=\partial r_{n,m}/\partial\Delta=\mathbb E_\gamma[A]$, the expected alignment under the Gibbs distribution $p_\gamma(A)\propto e^{-\langle A,\Delta\rangle/\gamma}$: $e_{i,j}$ is the probability that a random path visits cell $(i,j)$. Computing $\mathbb E_\gamma[A]$ directly costs $O(n^2m^2)$; the paper instead back-propagates through the recursion. Since $r_{i,j}$ only influences its three successors,
\begin{equation}
e_{i,j}=\sum_{(k,l)\in\{(i+1,j),(i,j+1),(i+1,j+1)\}} e_{k,l}\,\exp\!\big((r_{k,l}-r_{i,j}-\delta_{k,l})/\gamma\big),\qquad e_{n,m}=1,
\end{equation}
where each exponential is the softmax weight of $r_{i,j}$ inside $r_{k,l}$, read from the stored table at no extra log-sum-exp cost. For the squared Euclidean cost, $\nabla_{x_i}\sdtw=2\sum_j e_{i,j}(x_i-y_j)$: each $x_i$ is pulled towards the $y_j$ it is likely aligned with. As $\gamma\to0$, $E$ tends to the optimal DTW path $A^\star$, whose discontinuous dependence on $x$ is what soft-DTW removes (Fig.~\ref{fig:E}).

\paragraph{Learning with soft-DTW.}
The barycenter of $y_1,\dots,y_N$ solves $\min_{x\in\R^{n\times p}}\sum_i \frac{\lambda_i}{m_i}\sdtw(x,y_i)$ (Eq.~4 of the paper), a smooth but non-convex problem that we solve with L-BFGS. $k$-means alternates assignment and barycenter steps under $\sdtw$; the nearest-centroid classifier uses one barycenter per class; and for forecasting $\sdtw$ is the training loss of a network (our PyTorch \texttt{autograd.Function}).

\paragraph{A family of soft alignment distances (our extension).}
Soft-DTW is one instance of a soft dynamic program on the alignment grid~\cite{mensch2018differentiable}, in which each of the three edges entering cell $(i,j)$ has its own cost:
\begin{equation}
r_{i,j}=\minop\{r_{i-1,j-1}+c^{\searrow}_{i,j},\; r_{i-1,j}+c^{\downarrow}_{i,j},\; r_{i,j-1}+c^{\rightarrow}_{i,j}\}.
\end{equation}
The same backward pass returns the expected usage of every edge, from which the gradient w.r.t.\ $x$ follows by the chain rule. Choosing the edge costs gives: soft-DTW ($c=\delta_{i,j}$ for all edges); \emph{soft amerced DTW} (soft-ADTW), $c^{\downarrow}=c^{\rightarrow}=\delta_{i,j}+\omega$, a soft version of~\cite{herrmann2023amercing} where each warping step pays $\omega$; \emph{soft weighted DTW}, $c=w(|i-j|)\,\delta_{i,j}$ with a logistic weight~\cite{jeong2011weighted}; and \emph{soft ERP}~\cite{chen2004marriage}, where non-diagonal steps match a point with a gap. For soft-ADTW, $\omega=0$ recovers soft-DTW, $\omega\to\infty$ forbids warping (Euclidean distance for equal lengths), and $\partial r_{n,m}/\partial\omega$ is the expected number of warping steps. This matters because the entropic smoothing of soft-DTW carries a data-independent prior: for large $\gamma$, $E$ tends to the uniform average over paths, concentrated around the diagonal whatever the data (Fig.~\ref{fig:E}, right); step costs reshape this prior. Finally, each member $S$ can be debiased into the divergence $D(x,y)=S(x,y)-\tfrac12S(x,x)-\tfrac12S(y,y)$, which vanishes at $x=y$~\cite{blondel2021differentiable}.
