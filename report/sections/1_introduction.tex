\section{Introduction and contributions}

Comparing time series point by point (Euclidean distance) is fragile: two recordings of the same phenomenon are often shifted, locally stretched or of different lengths. Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)~\cite{sakoe1978dynamic} handles this by first finding the cheapest monotone \emph{alignment} between the two series. DTW is however a minimum over alignments, hence not differentiable, and its (sub)gradient jumps whenever the optimal alignment changes. This makes it a poor training loss, and averaging or clustering under DTW has relied on heuristics such as DBA~\cite{petitjean2011global}.

Cuturi and Blondel~\cite{cuturi2017soft} replace the minimum by a \emph{soft}-minimum, the same entropic smoothing that turns optimal transport into Sinkhorn distances~\cite{cuturi2013sinkhorn}. The resulting soft-DTW is smooth, has the same $O(nm)$ cost as DTW, and its gradient is obtained by a backward pass over Bellman's recursion. The paper uses it to compute barycenters, run $k$-means, classify with nearest centroids and train networks that output time series.

\paragraph{Contributions and work repartition.}
\textbf{Implementation:} we implemented soft-DTW from scratch (forward and backward recursions in Numba, a PyTorch loss with the hand-written backward, and an anti-diagonal PyTorch version that runs on GPU). No soft-DTW code was reused; \texttt{tslearn}~\cite{tavenard2020tslearn} is only used to load the UCR data, as the DBA baseline and as a numerical cross-check. The implementation is verified against brute-force enumeration of all alignments, finite differences and PyTorch autograd (85 unit tests).
\textbf{Reproduction:} we reproduce, on 10--13 UCR datasets, the paper's barycenter (Table 1), $k$-means (Table 2), nearest-centroid (Fig.~7) and forecasting (Table 3) experiments.
\textbf{Beyond the paper:} (i) we report magnitudes and computational costs that the paper omits, and add the baselines it does not compare to (Euclidean nearest centroid, 1-NN DTW); (ii) we show that soft-DTW is one instance of a soft dynamic program with edge costs~\cite{mensch2018differentiable}, implement it generically and derive from it soft versions of amerced DTW~\cite{herrmann2023amercing}, weighted DTW~\cite{jeong2011weighted} and ERP~\cite{chen2004marriage}, with their debiased divergences~\cite{blondel2021differentiable}; (iii) we compare this family with a neutral metric (1-NN accuracy, hyper-parameters chosen on validation).
